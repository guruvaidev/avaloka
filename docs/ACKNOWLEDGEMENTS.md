# The people this rests on

Avaloka is built on other people's ideas. Four of them shaped its design more
than most. Each entry below says what their work is, and where it applies what
we took from it, with links to their own pages or to primary sources.

Listing someone here records a debt, not an endorsement. It does not imply
that they know of this project or support it.

## Jim Gray

Jim Gray received the 1998 ACM A.M. Turing Award "for seminal contributions to
database and transaction processing research and technical leadership in system
implementation." In 2007 he described data-intensive science as a fourth
paradigm of discovery. *The Fourth Paradigm: Data-Intensive Scientific
Discovery* (Microsoft Research, 2009) collects essays that expand on that
vision. It is the argument the README opens with.

[Turing Award laureate page](https://amturing.acm.org/award_winners/gray_3649936.cfm) ·
[Microsoft Research home page](https://jimgray.azurewebsites.net/) ·
[*The Fourth Paradigm*](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/fourth-paradigm-data-intensive-scientific-discovery/)

## Martin Kleppmann

Martin Kleppmann is an Associate Professor at the University of Cambridge and
the author of *Designing Data-Intensive Applications* (O'Reilly). The second
edition, written with Chris Riccomini, appeared in 2026. With Adam Wiggins,
Peter van Hardenberg and Mark McGranaghan he wrote "Local-first software: You
own your data, in spite of the cloud" (Onward! 2019). The open-source
edition's promise is in the same spirit: it runs on your own hardware and stays
useful with no account, no key and no network.

[Home page](https://martin.kleppmann.com/) ·
[*Designing Data-Intensive Applications*](https://dataintensive.net/) ·
[Local-first software](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/local-first/) ·
[GitHub](https://github.com/ept)

## Michael Stonebraker

Michael Stonebraker received the 2014 ACM A.M. Turing Award "for fundamental
contributions to the concepts and practices underlying modern database
systems." His systems include Ingres; POSTGRES, the Berkeley project from which
PostgreSQL derives; and C-Store, a column-oriented DBMS. With Uğur Çetintemel
he wrote "'One size fits all': an idea whose time has come and gone" (ICDE
2005). Avaloka uses PostgreSQL, columnar Parquet files and a separate vector
store side by side, not one engine for everything.

[Turing Award laureate page](https://amturing.acm.org/award_winners/stonebraker_1172121.cfm) ·
[MIT CSAIL page](https://www.csail.mit.edu/person/michael-stonebraker) ·
[A brief history of PostgreSQL](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/history.html)

## Yann LeCun

Yann LeCun shared the 2018 ACM A.M. Turing Award with Yoshua Bengio and
Geoffrey Hinton "for conceptual and engineering breakthroughs that have made
deep neural networks a critical component of computing." His work on
convolutional networks for handwriting and document recognition dates from
1989 (*Neural Computation*) and 1998 (*Proceedings of the IEEE*). His recent
work argues for world models and joint-embedding architectures trained with
self-supervised learning.

[Turing Award laureate page](https://amturing.acm.org/award_winners/lecun_6017366.cfm) ·
[Home page](http://yann.lecun.com/)
